Licensing and insurance

Confirm the contractor carries liability insurance and is registered with WorkSafeBC â€” ask to see proof, not just take their word for it. This protects you if something goes wrong on site.

A written, itemized quote

A serious contractor gives you a written scope and fixed price, not a verbal ballpark. Vague quotes are where change-order surprises come from later.

Experience with your specific project type

New-home framing, renovation framing tying into old structure, and commercial tenant-improvement framing are genuinely different skill sets. Ask specifically about experience with a project like yours, not just general carpentry experience.

Permit and engineering comfort

A contractor who's comfortable coordinating permits and working from engineered drawings â€” and who tells you honestly when engineering is needed â€” is a good sign. One who suggests skipping permits on structural work is a red flag, full stop.

References and past work

Ask to see photos of similar completed projects, and if possible, talk to a past client about their experience with schedule, communication and follow-through after the job was done.
