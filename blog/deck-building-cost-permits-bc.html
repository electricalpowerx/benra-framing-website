What drives deck framing cost

The main factors are size, height off grade (elevated decks need more structure and often railings), number of levels, stair complexity, and footing type â€” helical piles install faster than poured concrete in some conditions but cost more per footing. Ledger attachment complexity and site access also matter.

When do you need a permit?

Most Lower Mainland municipalities require a permit for decks above a certain height (often around 24 inches) or attached to the house, though exact rules vary by city. We confirm your municipality's specific requirement before quoting so there are no surprises.

The structural details that actually matter

A deck that lasts comes down to a few unglamorous details: footings sized to the actual load and soil, a ledger board that's bolted and properly flashed (not just nailed through siding), and joist spacing matched to your decking material. These aren't upgrades â€” they're the minimum for a code-compliant, safe deck.

Decking material vs. structure

The framing (footings, posts, beams, joists) is a separate cost from the decking surface (wood, composite, etc.) on top. We frame to the spacing your chosen decking material requires, whether you're supplying it or we are.
